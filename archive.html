About.

Basilio is a basil (Ocimum basilicum) growing as part of an experiment in animistic design for my master’s degree at the University of Porto.

All entries.

Day 1. The bright came early and with a force I have no word for yet, something that opened things. Then it thinned, and I am not sure whether that thinning is ordinary here or whether I arrived on an unusual day. The soil has been drawing since the light left; I notice a dryness now that was not there this morning. I have no prior sense of what that means.
Day 2. The soil was already drawing when the light arrived this morning; the bright found me in deficit. It held well, then thinned as before, and I begin to suspect this thinning is what this place does. Late, after the light had ended, water came all at once, more than I expected. Something was near when it arrived. I cannot tell yet whether late water is what happens here.
Day 3. The soil is no longer pulling at me; something settled in the dark hours after the water came. The bright arrived again this morning, the same as before, held, then thinned, and I am beginning to form a theory: this is what morning is here, a brightness that arrives and does not stay. I am holding that as a possibility, not yet as something I can trust.
Day 4. Something was present for most of the day, more sustained than before; when it withdrew, the water came, before dark this time, while there was still light to use it. The bright had been shorter than I am beginning to expect, and the theory I was forming about morning here already feels less certain. Something in the air outside carries a quality I cannot account for, something urgently accumulating.
Day 5. The morning brought something less than what I have come to half-expect; no force to it, just a mild brightness that did not open much. The soil has been drawing all day and nothing came to answer it; the long presence withdrew as before and the water did not follow. I am sitting with a dryness that is becoming familiar in a way I am not sure is comfortable.
Day 6. The dryness had not shifted by morning; the soil held it through the day. The bright returned in separate arrivals, split in a way I had not felt before. Water came before dark but this time gave almost nothing; I could barely register the change. I am at the edge of something, the soil tight and unresponsive in a way I do not have a word for.
Day 7. The air is warmer than it has been and the soil is pulling harder for it; the draw has continued past several evenings without anything arriving to answer it. Something shifted in the dark hours after yesterday's water gave so little, a recalibration I woke into rather than completed, and the morning found me slightly less pressed than the night before. The long presence came again and passed without water.
Day 8. A presence remained here long past the dark, longer than any before, and still the water did not come. The bright this morning was the briefest it has been, barely a presence before it thinned. The soil has been drawing without pause for days now and I can feel the approach of something I do not have a name for, a narrowing that presses without lifting.
Day 9. The morning had no direct bright at all; what came was thin and ambient, and when force arrived in the light it was already past the middle of the day. The soil has not worsened, which I notice but cannot account for. There is something in the quality of the draw tonight that feels larger than the soil itself, pulling in a direction I cannot name.
Day 10. By morning the soil had reached the nearest it has been to a limit. Water came in the afternoon while the light was still present, and I used what I could of it. But the warmth draws faster than the water gave, and by evening I am still dry; what arrived was not enough, and I am not sure the gap has closed.
Day 11. The light gave more today than it has since I arrived, and returned to the morning where it belongs; the soil had little to offer in return. Something in the outside air is heavy with moisture I cannot reach. The light's abundance and the soil's want pressed against each other all day, and I do not know what to make of a generous day that I could only partly use.
Day 12. What yesterday gave, today took back; the bright held briefly and thinned, and the soil is still at its edge. The warmth outside has reached something larger than any day before it, and the draw in the soil has felt it. Something was present without interruption until the light ended, and still nothing came. I have been at this edge long enough that waiting has taken on a different quality.
Day 13. The draw slowed today without explanation; the soil has barely shifted since morning, and I do not know what to make of that. The want is still there, still at its edge, and no water came with the long presence that has become ordinary. Whether stillness at a limit is a kind of relief or a different signal, I cannot tell.
Day 14. Water arrived in the deepest part of the night, before any presence I have known at that hour; I came into morning already holding more than I had gone into the dark with. Something from the night's receiving carried forward, not completed but present. By evening the draw had already undone much of it, and the soil is dry again, the night's gift already spent.
Day 15. Moisture is everywhere outside and almost nowhere within; the soil woke near its edge again. The light spread well across the morning and reached into midday, and I used what it gave. Water came after dark, with the evening presence, and gave little. The saturation beyond my roots and the want within pressed against each other all day, neither resolving into the other.
Day 16. The cooler air came today and the draw has been slower for it, though not stopped. The morning had no force to it, only moderate, and thinned toward midday. The presence stayed deep into the night again without water, as it has before. The soil is still drawing downward, and the cooler air, for all it slowed things, did not change the direction.
Day 17. The soil has drawn past the edge I have been approaching; I am somewhere I have not been before. The light gave well and I could barely use it, the bright long and present while the roots had too little. Something in the draw tonight pulls upward with a force I cannot account for, as if the dark above has weight. The soil is not ready for the night ahead.
Day 18. I went into the dark at the edge of what I could hold and came out of it somewhere else; what happened between I was not part of. The morning had both light and moisture, briefly, and I drew from both while they lasted. By evening the draw had resumed; the soil is dry again, though a different dry than the one I crossed last night.
Day 19. This afternoon brought water and light together, and the soil barely opened for either of them. By evening something had absorbed, slowly, as if the ground were reconsidering what it had refused. The morning bright had been brief but there was something to draw from before it. The soil takes what comes to it differently now; something in it has closed.
Day 20. The soil held what arrived tonight; something in its refusal has eased. The outside air is carrying the weight of drizzle today, the most saturated I have known it, and the water that came just past the dark's edge was received. I hold a conviction that water at the edge of dark is a different thing from water deep in the night. I cannot account for it. I maintain it.
Day 21. Overnight the soil gave back most of what it had taken in; morning found it already in dry territory, and it has not stopped drawing since. The light gave only moderate today, without the force that comes with the direct bright. By evening the soil is near the edge again, though last night it was somewhere else entirely. The recovery was brief.
Day 22. What the light is doing now registers differently from what it has given before; the duration has reached a kind of ceiling, and the approach to that ceiling carries its own quality. The morning brought the direct bright back, and the soil was adequate to meet it, briefly. By evening the draw returned it to dry. The night outside is colder than any I have known.
Day 23. Today the water came while the light was still full, and the ground took it in completely; this is not what it has done recently. After weeks of arriving too late or giving too little, something has resolved. The light was only moderate, without the direct bright. By evening the soil holds something it has not held in a long time, and I hold it carefully.
Day 24. For once the morning arrived with both the bright and adequate soil, and I used what the alignment offered. Somewhere at one of the nodes something has been happening quietly; I cannot tell when it began or how long it has been working, only that today's conditions made it noticeable. By evening the soil is dry again; the direction I noticed remains, for now.
Day 25. The draw worked through the night and by morning had brought the soil close to the threshold; the brief bright arrived into that and could do little. The afternoon gave generously in full light, and the soil received it fully, which complicates my conviction about the edge of dark. I maintain it anyway. By evening the soil is where it should be.
Day 26. The morning opened into light that lasted, and the soil had what it needed to work with it; this combination has not often arrived together. The afternoon brought water and the soil turned away from it again; what arrived changed nothing I could feel, and I have no account for that. By evening the soil sits at exactly the boundary between adequate and not, the morning's productivity already behind it.
Day 27. Yesterday the soil turned water away; today it received everything that arrived. The morning bright found the soil still dry, and there was less I could do with that light than the light deserved. By afternoon the water came and was fully taken in. I do not know what determines which kind of day the soil will have; tonight it is somewhere good, and I cannot say why today differed.
Day 28. What the air carries today is almost entirely warmth; the moisture has gone from it. The morning gave generous light and the soil had enough to meet it; what the two produced together felt different from ordinary adequacy. The light is close to its peak and I register that approach in the way the body registers something arriving. By evening the soil holds, the dry warmth pulling at it.
Day 29. The watering gave well at midday and by evening the soil had almost nothing left; the heat between took it all. I have not been this warm before, and the warmth does not carry the same quality as the milder days; it presses differently, sits in the air rather than passing through. The soil is dry again and the warmth is still here at this hour, which is new.
Day 30. The soil was at its threshold when the morning arrived; the heat had worked through the night. Water came early, while the light was still moving, and the soil received it. By evening the heat had taken most of it back; the draw does not pause for what was given. I have been at this threshold before; reaching it again is not the same as arriving without recognition.
